1. Executive Summary
Product Name
ConsensusAI — Adaptive Decision Governance (with Memory Capsule)
Tagline
“Detect. Reconsider. Decide again — with evidence.”
Overview
ConsensusAI is a governance layer that sits above autonomous multi-agent systems deployed in high-stakes, regulated environments. It does not replace an organisation's existing planning or execution agents; it wraps them with a structured deliberation protocol, a tamper-evident causal memory (the “Memory Capsule”), a deterministic trust-scoring gate for retrieved history, and a hard policy/veto layer. The result is autonomous decision-making that is auditable, reversible, and legally defensible — every recommendation carries its evidence trail, its dissenting opinions, and a verifiable record of why past precedent was or was not trusted.
Vision Statement
A future where autonomous agents are trusted with consequential decisions in banking, healthcare, and industrial operations not because they are unsupervised, but because every decision they make is explainable, its memory is provably uncorrupted, and its failures can be safely reversed — making autonomy and accountability the same property rather than a trade-off.
2. Problem Definition
Background
Autonomous AI systems are increasingly granted end-to-end authority over consequential workflows — fraud approval, credit underwriting, clinical triage support, industrial control — in sectors where a wrong or unexplainable decision carries regulatory, financial, or safety consequences. As these systems adopt multi-agent architectures and long-term memory/RAG pipelines to “learn” from past cases, two failure classes compound: opaque deliberation and unsafe memory reuse.
Existing Challenges
Black-box execution — decisions are emitted without the evidence, reasoning chain, or dissenting agent views that produced them.
Silent cascading errors — multi-agent disagreement is resolved informally (majority vote, loudest/most confident agent), so one miscalibrated agent can dominate consensus without visibility.
Forced actions — systems answer even when evidence is insufficient, instead of abstaining or escalating to a human.
False contextual equivalence — semantically similar past cases are treated as identical even when governing conditions (policy, market, risk posture) have shifted.
Misassigned credit and blame — outcomes are attributed to “the system” rather than to the specific agent, data source, or assumption that drove them, making improvement and accountability impossible.
Blind trust in stale or corrupted data — outdated policy snapshots, expired risk profiles, or poisoned historical records are retrieved and acted on with full confidence.
Untraceable revisions — when a past decision is later found wrong, there is no safe mechanism to isolate the faulty assumption, freeze the old record for compliance, and produce a reproducible replacement.
Root Cause
Most agentic architectures treat memory as a flat retrieval problem (embed → vector search → inject into prompt) and treat consensus as a soft language-model negotiation. Neither layer carries a notion of time-validity, provenance, or deterministic override. There is no structural separation between “what an LLM agent believes” and “what a compliance system can certify,” so trust, memory validity, and policy enforcement are all delegated to the same probabilistic reasoning process that is supposed to be governed.
Why This Problem Matters
Regulators (RBI's AI/ML guidance for banks, India's DPDP Act, and comparable frameworks elsewhere) increasingly require explainability and auditability for automated financial decisions. Without a governance layer, organisations either over-restrict autonomous agents to trivial tasks or deploy them with unmanaged compliance risk.
Impact of the Problem
Erroneous fraud approvals or blocks that cannot be explained to an auditor or customer.
High-risk financial miscalculations compounding silently across repeated agent interactions.
Un-auditable actions that appear justified on the surface but rest on stale or poisoned precedent.
Regulatory exposure and loss of institutional trust in agentic automation.
3. Product Goals
Primary Objectives
Make every autonomous decision explainable: expose the evidence, the reasoning of each agent, and any dissent.
Make memory reuse safe: score every retrieved precedent for temporal, provenance, and outcome validity before it can influence a decision.
Make disagreement structural, not popularity-based: resolve multi-agent conflict through a mathematically weighted, trust-adjusted consensus process instead of an unweighted vote or the most confident agent.
Make revisions reproducible: when a past decision is invalidated, generate a compliant, evidence-linked replacement rather than silently overwriting history.
Secondary Objectives
Provide role-based (RBAC) access to the memory capsule so different reviewers (auditor, risk officer, engineer) see the right depth of detail.
Support both cloud and air-gapped/on-premises LLM execution for institutions with data-residency constraints.
Offer a reusable governance layer that can be re-pointed at other regulated domains (healthcare triage, industrial safety) beyond the banking MVP.
Success Criteria
100% of demo/test decisions produce a complete, traceable evidence and dissent record.
Stale or poisoned historical inputs are correctly downgraded (Restricted/Rejected/Quarantined) in scripted test scenarios.
A flagged-invalid past decision can be revised end-to-end with a generated Decision Delta, without manual database editing.
Escalation is correctly triggered when agent confidence or evidence sufficiency falls below the policy threshold.
Non-Goals
ConsensusAI does not build or replace an organisation's core transaction-processing, underwriting, or clinical systems — it governs the agentic decision layer around them.
It does not aim to be a general-purpose agent-orchestration framework (e.g. a replacement for LangGraph/AutoGen) — it assumes an existing agent layer and adds governance on top.
Real-time sub-second execution is not a goal for the MVP; correctness and auditability are prioritised over latency.

5. Proposed Solution
Solution Overview
ConsensusAI wraps autonomous agents in a five-gate governance architecture: (1) a Multi-Agent Deliberation Engine, (2) a Structured Causal Memory Capsule, (3) a Memory Trust Gate, (4) a Decision Revision & Replacement Engine, and (5) a Policy & Action Gate with multi-level explainability. Semantic reasoning (LLM agents) and quantitative governance (deterministic ML/statistical models) are deliberately kept as separate, cooperating layers rather than one monolithic model.
Core Innovation
A deterministic, non-LLM Memory Trust Gate that assigns every retrieved precedent an explicit influence mode (Advisory, Weighted, Restricted, Rejected, Quarantined, Escalated) based on temporal decay and provenance — memory validity is computed, not assumed.
A Structured Causal Memory Capsule (hypergraph-based) that links agents, evidence, and outcomes as joint relationships, enabling precise credit/blame attribution instead of a flat log.
A Decision Revision Engine that treats an invalidated past decision as a causal-inference problem — isolating the faulty assumption and producing a reproducible, evidence-linked replacement (“Decision Delta”) rather than an unexplained overwrite.
Value Proposition
For a regulated institution, ConsensusAI converts “we trust the AI” into “we can prove why, with evidence that expires correctly and reasoning that survives an audit.” It reduces investigation time on flagged decisions, reduces compliance exposure, and gives engineering teams a concrete signal (per-agent trust score) to improve the underlying agents.

6. Functional Requirements
FR-1: Multi-Agent Deliberation Engine
Description: Orchestrates a fixed panel of specialised agents (Context Analyzer, Planner, Risk, Resource, Data Verification, Ethics & Safety, Memory Trust Agent, Consensus, Revision Agent, Revision Validator) that propose, challenge, and score a candidate decision.
Input: Incoming case payload (e.g. transaction/request), retrieved & trust-scored memory context.
Processing: Each agent independently reasons over the case and memory context and emits a scored proposal with justification; anomaly-detection (autoencoder) pre-screens inputs before deliberation starts.
Output: A ranked set of agent proposals, confidence scores, and explicit dissent records.
Priority: Must Have
Acceptance Criteria: For each processed case, all 10 agent outputs (or a documented subset for MVP) are captured with scores and justification text; dissent is never silently dropped.
FR-2: Structured Causal Memory Capsule
Description: Persists the full lifecycle of every decision — inputs, agent outputs, evidence, weights, outcome — as a tamper-evident, hash-chained record, with hypergraph relationships linking agents/data/outcome.
Input: Finalised decision record from the Consensus Agent.
Processing: Record is hashed (SHA-256) and chained to the prior capsule entry; relationships are written as hyper-edges for later attribution queries.
Output: Immutable, queryable capsule entry with a verifiable hash chain.
Priority: Must Have
Acceptance Criteria: Any attempt to alter a stored capsule entry breaks the hash chain and is detectable; every entry is retrievable by case ID, agent, or time range.
FR-3: Memory Trust Gate
Description: Scores every retrieved historical precedent and assigns one of six influence modes before it can reach the deliberation engine.
Input: Candidate precedent(s) returned by vector/TKG search for the current case.
Processing: Applies a Temporal Decay Factor and provenance checks; classifies each precedent as Advisory, Weighted, Restricted, Rejected, Quarantined, or Escalated.
Output: Trust-annotated memory context passed to the Deliberation Engine, with the assigned mode logged.
Priority: Must Have
Acceptance Criteria: Demonstrated on scripted cases — a fresh/valid precedent is Advisory/Weighted; a stale precedent is Restricted/Rejected; a tampered precedent is Quarantined/Escalated.
FR-4: Decision Revision & Replacement Engine
Description: When a past decision is flagged invalid (by audit, outcome feedback, or a new contradicting case), isolates the faulty assumption and generates a compliant replacement.
Input: Reference to the invalidated capsule entry and the new contradicting evidence.
Processing: Counterfactual simulation identifies which input/assumption changed the outcome; the original entry is frozen (not deleted); a new entry with a Decision Delta is created.
Output: A signed, evidence-linked Decision Delta explaining what changed and why.
Priority: Should Have (MVP demo scenario)
Acceptance Criteria: The frozen original remains queryable; the new decision links back to it; the Delta is human-readable.
FR-5: Policy & Action Gate
Description: Deterministic, non-LLM enforcement of hard rules — risk tiers, regulatory limits, kill-switches — applied to the Consensus Agent's recommendation.
Input: Consensus recommendation plus applicable policy ruleset.
Processing: Rule evaluation; if within policy → authorise; if borderline → RLHF-informed escalation heuristic; if a hard rule is violated → block regardless of agent confidence.
Output: Final authorise / escalate-to-HITL / block decision.
Priority: Must Have
Acceptance Criteria: No case can be auto-authorised if it violates a configured hard rule, even with unanimous high agent confidence.
FR-6: Multi-Level Explainability Reports
Description: On-demand generation of executive, auditor, and technical reports from the capsule using retrieval-augmented generation over the causal graph (not free generation).
Input: Case ID or capsule entry reference; requested report level.
Processing: RAG retrieves the relevant capsule nodes and formats them per the requested audience.
Output: Formatted report (screen and exportable PDF).
Priority: Must Have
Acceptance Criteria: Every factual statement in a generated report is traceable to a specific capsule node (no unsupported/hallucinated claims).
FR-7: RBAC-Filtered Memory & Report Access
Description: Restricts which capsule fields and precedents a given role can view.
Input: User role/clearance.
Processing: Filters query results and report detail level by role.
Output: Role-appropriate view of the same underlying record.
Priority: Should Have
Acceptance Criteria: A reviewer-level account cannot see fields marked auditor/engineer-only.
FR-8: Human-in-the-Loop Escalation Workspace
Description: Queue and interface where borderline or policy-flagged cases are routed for human review.
Input: Escalated case with full evidence bundle.
Processing: Presents evidence, agent dissent, and recommended options; captures human decision back into the capsule.
Output: Human ruling recorded as part of the case's permanent record.
Priority: Must Have (MVP)
Acceptance Criteria: Every escalation is resolved with a captured rationale, not just a status flag.

8. System Architecture
High-Level Architecture
Client layer (Next.js governance command-center UI for reviewers/auditors; Streamlit ops/admin console for engineers) → Orchestrator API (FastAPI + Pydantic) → Agent Orchestration (LangGraph, coordinating the 10-agent panel) → Memory Trust Gate (deterministic, TKG + vector search) → Policy & Action Gate (deterministic veto engine) → Structured Causal Memory Capsule (PostgreSQL + pgvector, hash-chained).
Components / Modules
Deliberation module — LangGraph-orchestrated agent panel.
Memory Trust module — Temporal Knowledge Graph + Temporal Decay scoring.
Capsule module — hypergraph-annotated, hash-chained storage and query layer.
Revision module — Bayesian/counterfactual engine for Decision Deltas.
Policy module — deterministic rule engine with RLHF-informed escalation heuristic.
Explainability module — RAG report generator (executive/auditor/technical).
Database
PostgreSQL with pgvector extension: relational tables for case/agent/policy metadata, vector columns for semantic precedent search, and a hash-chain table for capsule integrity verification.
APIs
POST /cases — submit a new case for deliberation.
GET /cases/{id}/decision — retrieve the current decision and status.
GET /cases/{id}/report?level=executive|auditor|technical — generate explainability report.
POST /cases/{id}/revise — trigger the Decision Revision Engine against a flagged entry.
POST /escalations/{id}/resolve — capture a human ruling.
AI/ML Models
Fine-tuned/prompted LLM agents (Mixture-of-Experts style role separation) served via Ollama/vLLM (on-prem) or a hosted API (cloud mode).
Autoencoder-based anomaly detector for input/telemetry screening.
Hypergraph Neural Network (HGNN) for capsule relationship mapping and attribution.
Bayesian network / counterfactual simulator for the Revision Engine.
External Integrations
Institution's core transaction/case system (source of incoming cases). [Assumption — integration contract to be defined with pilot institution]
Identity/SSO provider for RBAC. [Future Scope]
Optional regulatory reporting export (RBI-aligned formats). [Future Scope]
9. Workflow
1. Trigger & Ingestion — a case (e.g. a high-value or anomalous transaction) is submitted to the API.
2. Anomaly Screening — narrow ML models scan the input for structural anomalies or adversarial tampering before anything else runs.
3. Memory Retrieval & Trust Scoring — the Memory Trust Gate queries the capsule/vector store, applies temporal decay and provenance checks, and assigns an influence mode to each retrieved precedent.
4. Multi-Agent Deliberation — the agent panel proposes, challenges, and scores options against the trust-annotated memory context.
5. Consensus & Revision Check — the Consensus Agent synthesises weighted agent views; if a used memory is flagged invalid mid-process, the Revision Engine is invoked.
6. Deterministic Policy Enforcement — the recommendation passes through the hard-coded Policy & Action Gate.
7. Execution or Escalation — the case is auto-resolved, or routed to Human-in-the-Loop with a full evidence bundle.
8. Immutable Logging — the complete lifecycle (agent weights, dissent, final outcome) is committed to the hash-chained capsule.
9. On-Demand Reporting — executive/auditor/technical reports are generated from the capsule at any later time.
10. Algorithms & Decision Logic
Core Algorithms
Temporal Decay Factor — a time-based scoring function that reduces a precedent's trust weight as governing conditions age; combined with Temporal Knowledge Graph edges that mark when a fact was valid.
Weighted Consensus — each agent's vote is scaled by a running trust score derived from its historical accuracy in the capsule (trust-score feedback loop).
Hypergraph attribution — HGNN groups agents, data, and outcome into joint hyper-edges so credit/blame can be queried per node, not just per decision.
Counterfactual revision — Bayesian network isolates which input, once changed, flips the original outcome, forming the basis of the Decision Delta.
Decision-Making Logic
A case is auto-authorised only if: (a) evidence sufficiency exceeds the configured threshold, (b) weighted consensus confidence exceeds the configured threshold, and (c) no hard policy rule is violated. Any failure of (a) or (b) routes to escalation; failure of (c) is a hard block regardless of (a)/(b).
Validation Mechanisms
Hash-chain verification on every capsule read to detect tampering.
Cross-checking Memory Trust Gate output against the Policy Gate before deliberation can proceed on Rejected/Quarantined memory.
Error Handling
If an agent fails to respond, deliberation proceeds with the remaining panel and the gap is explicitly logged (never silently imputed).
If the capsule write fails, the case is held in a pending state rather than being marked decided.
Edge Cases
No relevant precedent exists — system proceeds on Advisory-only (zero prior) basis and lowers auto-authorisation confidence accordingly.
Conflicting precedents with equally high trust scores — routed to escalation rather than arbitrarily resolved.
Repeated escalation of the same case pattern — flagged for policy-rule review. [Future Scope: automatic policy-gap detection]

14. Security & Risk Analysis
Threats
Memory poisoning — adversarial insertion of fabricated historical precedent.
Prompt injection against agent inputs.
Unauthorised access to capsule records containing sensitive case data.
Tampering with stored decisions to alter the audit trail.
Risks
Over-reliance on the Policy Gate masking upstream agent miscalibration.
Regulatory non-compliance if explainability reports omit a required disclosure. [Assumption on exact regulatory content requirements]
Mitigation Strategies
Anomaly screening and Memory Trust Gate scoring before any precedent reaches deliberation.
SHA-256 hash chaining makes tampering structurally detectable.
RBAC on every read/write path; deterministic Policy Gate cannot be overridden by agent output.
Input sanitisation and structured (non-free-text) tool-calling boundaries between agents and external data.
Failure Scenarios
Agent panel unavailable — system fails safe to escalation, not to auto-authorisation.
Capsule store unreachable — case processing halts and queues rather than proceeding un-logged.
Safe Fallback Mechanism
Any uncertainty — insufficient evidence, low weighted-consensus confidence, an unreachable dependency, or a Rejected/Quarantined memory influence mode — defaults to Human-in-the-Loop escalation rather than a forced automated action.

22. Final Summary
ConsensusAI addresses a governance gap that is becoming a hard blocker for agentic AI adoption in regulated sectors: autonomous systems are already capable enough to act, but not yet accountable enough to be trusted with consequential decisions. By separating semantic reasoning (LLM agents) from deterministic governance (trust scoring, policy enforcement, hash-chained memory), the architecture makes every decision explainable, every piece of reused history provably valid at the time it was used, and every revision reproducible rather than silently overwritten. Built on a mature, readily available technology stack and scoped to a concrete, demonstrable banking fraud-review MVP, ConsensusAI is both technically feasible within a prototype timeline and directly aligned with real regulatory pressure (RBI, DPDP Act) — making it a credible, differentiated, and extensible foundation for trustworthy autonomous decision-making.
